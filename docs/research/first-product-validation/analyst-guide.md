# Manual analyst guide

Status: Frozen with protocol version 1.0

The analyst's job is to test whether a defensible recurring decision pattern exists. The job is not to produce the most persuasive story.

## Rules

- Start with objective chess evidence.
- Separate fact, observation, pattern, hypothesis, and intervention.
- Prefer repeated, comparable decisions over isolated centipawn loss.
- Search for contradictory examples before selecting a diagnosis.
- Record rejected candidate patterns and why they failed.
- Consider simpler explanations, including missing chess knowledge, time pressure, attention, and execution failure.
- Avoid psychological diagnoses, personality claims, and unsupported causal language.
- Do not treat the player's rating or opening preference as a diagnosis.
- Do not cherry-pick positions or silently discard inconvenient examples.
- Do not force a pattern. Report insufficient evidence.
- Use engine output as chess evidence, not as evidence of what the player was thinking.
- Keep PLAYER SELF-REPORT separate from game-derived observations. When practical, form the initial game-derived candidates before reviewing the self-report, then use the self-report as context and a comparison point.
- Produce no more than one primary diagnostic hypothesis.
- Make the intervention bounded and traceable to the selected pattern.

## Required reasoning record

For every selected pattern, record:

1. The comparable decision context.
2. The objective evidence at each supporting position.
3. The observable common behavior.
4. At least one competing explanation when relevant.
5. Contradictory or disconfirming examples.
6. Why the evidence state is insufficient, candidate, supported, or strongly supported.
7. Why the intervention follows from the evidence.

## Language check

Prefer:

> The evidence currently supports the hypothesis that the player often commits before checking forcing replies.

Avoid:

> The player does not understand tactics.

The first sentence can be revised by later evidence. The second overclaims a cause and a stable trait.

## Reviewer handoff

A second reviewer should be able to identify the supporting positions, understand why they form a group, see what evidence was rejected, and explain where the diagnosis remains uncertain. If that is not possible, downgrade the evidence state or report insufficient evidence.
